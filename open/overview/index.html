Abstract, as published

The Grothendieckian Programme is a series of seven works on machine reasoning about mathematics (34 volumes, 26,021 pages), released openly on Zenodo in October 2026 with an overview volume. Two of the works specify a reasoning architecture — a core that holds mathematical worlds as typed, versioned and verifiable objects, and a kernel that reasons inside such worlds by sites, sheaves and descent. Five apply one method, recursive mathematical discovery, to the Riemann Hypothesis, the conjecture of Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer, the Hodge conjecture, the abc conjecture and the Langlands programme, and record what it proves, what it reduces to stated hypotheses and what it leaves open. This paper describes the architecture, the five research subjects, the method, the published corpus and the open process by which the works are to be reviewed and continued. None of the works claims a proof of any of these conjectures; they are first drafts and have not been peer-reviewed.

Use of AI tools

The main ideas and the architecture of the Grothendieckian Programme are the author's and come from the author's research at WiseMotif.ai. In the seven works, AI tools assisted with drafting and with part of the literature collation; all of the text was reviewed by the author. Lean verification of the seven works is incomplete. Because of their size, the works are first drafts (Research Architecture Edition 1) and have not been peer-reviewed; they need review by experts and further work on the programme. They are released openly for that purpose, in the hope of further contributions and of more recursive mathematical discovery in AI for mathematics. For the present paper, statements were extracted from the published works by script and checked against the PDFs; AI tools assisted with drafting and with literature collation; the author reviewed every statement.
