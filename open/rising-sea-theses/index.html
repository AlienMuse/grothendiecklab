Abstract, as published

The Grothendieckian Programme is a set of seven works on machine reasoning about mathematics (34 volumes, 26,021 pages), released openly in October 2026 as first drafts that have not been peer-reviewed. This paper draws from them twelve theses on how mathematics and machine reasoning should be combined. Each thesis is stated in one sentence, argued, tied to the passages of the seven works on which it rests, related to existing work, and paired with an observation that would refute it. The theses concern the unit of mathematical knowledge for a reasoning system, the place of epistemic status, the latent space of mathematical reasoning, attention, failure, authority, and the method of discovery. The paper adds no mathematical result to the seven works; none of them claims a proof of the Riemann Hypothesis, the Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture, the Hodge conjecture, the abc conjecture or the Langlands correspondence.

The twelve theses

  1. THESIS 1 Worlds, not isolated theorems

    For a reasoning system, the unit of mathematical knowledge is a typed, verifiable world, not an isolated theorem.

    What would refute it →

  2. THESIS 2 Status is part of the type

    The epistemic status of a statement belongs to its type.

    What would refute it →

  3. THESIS 3 A structured latent space

    Mathematical reasoning needs a structured, non-vectorial latent space.

    What would refute it →

  4. THESIS 4 Attention as descent

    Attention can be rebuilt as descent.

    What would refute it →

  5. THESIS 5 Report failure; abstain without evidence

    A reasoning system should report where local information fails to glue, and abstain when independent overlap data are absent.

    What would refute it →

  6. THESIS 6 A chain of non-implications

    Operational completion does not imply mathematical validity, which does not imply epistemic promotion, which does not imply publication.

    What would refute it →

  7. THESIS 7 Machines propose; humans authorize

    Machines may propose; humans authorize publication.

    What would refute it →

  8. THESIS 8 The rising sea

    Hard problems are better approached by raising the surrounding theory than by direct attack.

    What would refute it →

  9. THESIS 9 State what is not established

    A result is not complete until it states what it does not establish.

    What would refute it →

  10. THESIS 10 Discovery is recursive

    Mathematical discovery is recursive: propose, test against what is known, abstract what survives, ask again.

    What would refute it →

  11. THESIS 11 Creating language

    A mathematical reasoning system must be able to create notation and language, not only use them.

    What would refute it →

  12. THESIS 12 Judge a method by its open ledger

    A method should be judged by an open ledger of what it establishes, what it reduces to stated hypotheses, and what it leaves open.

Use of AI tools

The main ideas and the architecture of the Grothendieckian Programme are the author's and come from the author's research at WiseMotif.ai. In the seven works, AI tools assisted with drafting and with part of the literature collation; all of the text was reviewed by the author. Lean verification of the seven works is incomplete. Because of their size, the works are first drafts (Research Architecture Edition 1) and have not been peer-reviewed; they need review by experts and further work on the programme. They are released openly for that purpose, in the hope of further contributions and of more recursive mathematical discovery in AI for mathematics. For the present paper, statements were extracted from the published works by script and checked against the PDFs; AI tools assisted with drafting and with literature collation; the author selected the theses and problems and reviewed every statement.
