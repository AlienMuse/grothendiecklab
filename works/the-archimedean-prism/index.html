Abstract, as published

The Archimedean Prism is the fifth work in the WiseMotif Monograph of Mathematical Intelligence. It belongs to the Grothendieckian Programme as a self-contained system and as one of the Programme's research subjects: a problem on which the method of recursive mathematical discovery is carried through in full, in the manner of Grothendieck's rising sea. The problem is the Hodge Conjecture, and the question is why it is known in codimension one, in codimension n−1, in dimension at most three and for isolated families such as cubic fourfolds, and nowhere else; and what kind of object would make algebraicity of a Hodge class a statement that can be tested.

The diagnosis is negative and exact. Voisin's 2002 counterexample shows that the conjecture is false for compact Kähler manifolds, and the examples of Atiyah–Hirzebruch and Kollár show that its integral form is false. Any correct mechanism must therefore consume projectivity at an identifiable step and produce denominators at an identifiable step. The proposal changes the object. Built on o-minimal geometry — definable Chow, definable GAGA and the theorem of Bakker–Klingler–Tsimerman — the archimedean prism Δ(X) is a definable bi-Rees deformation whose four distinguished fibres are the Hodge decomposition, its conjugate, and the Betti cohomology with its rational lattice. Algebraicity becomes a descent datum along the prism, governed by twelve axioms AP1–AP12, and the Hodge Conjecture becomes the vanishing of an obstruction class.

The master theorem proves, from the ten unconditional axioms AP1–AP10, that five formulations stand or fall together: the Hodge Conjecture, the Rigidification Conjecture, the Concentration Principle, the Trace–Hodge Conjecture, and the split into Hodgic Fixed-Point Descent and Algebraic Seam Recognition; the Universal Kuga–Satake Conjecture reduces the problem to abelian varieties, itself open. The equivalence proves none of its statements. Of nine obstructions O1–O9, seven are discharged, refined or shown to lie off the main line, and two are live: the existence of a rigidification datum for a single Hodge class, and the passage from a closed to a positive closed current of integral type, the unique point at which projectivity is consumed. A seven-layer computational system, POLARIS-HC, is specified in full, with certified periods and PSLQ/LLL relations treated as candidates, never as proofs; a Lean 4 proof-obligation ledger records that the conjecture cannot yet be stated in mathlib, and its fragments are published as non-compiling design targets. The work closes with eleven falsifiers, a verdict against seven criteria fixed in advance — four met, two partially, one not met: a proof of the Hodge Conjecture — and sixty problems.

Every substantive assertion carries one of four status labels — known, conditional, new, open — where it is made. The Hodge conjecture remains open. This work does not prove it, and proves no case of it that was not already known.
